# Copywriting

Use this skill to produce marketing copy that is clear, specific, credible, customer-centered, and oriented toward a defined action.

## Before Writing

First identify the information already available in the conversation, project files, product brief, or marketing context. Do not ask for information that is already available.

Establish:

1. **Page or asset purpose** — landing page, homepage, ad, quiz, campaign, pricing, product section, etc.
2. **Primary action** — the single action the reader should take.
3. **Audience** — who the message is for and what problem they are trying to solve.
4. **Desired outcome** — what changes for the customer after acting.
5. **Objections** — doubts, risks, complexity, price, credibility, or inertia.
6. **Traffic/context** — where the visitor came from and what they already know.
7. **Proof** — only use real facts, testimonials, numbers, guarantees, credentials, or results supplied by the user or verified from an appropriate source.

## Core Principles

### Clarity over cleverness
The reader should understand the offer and next step immediately. Prefer plain language over slogans that require interpretation.

### Benefits over features
Translate what the product does into what that means for the customer.

**Feature:** guided prompts for building a website.  
**Benefit:** the customer knows what to ask the AI at each stage instead of starting from a blank screen.

### Specificity over vague marketing
Avoid empty claims such as "innovative", "revolutionary", "complete solution", "take your business to the next level", or similar phrases unless they are backed by concrete meaning.

### Customer language over company language
Use the words the audience naturally uses to describe the problem, desired result, objections, and decision.

### One idea per section
Every section should advance one clear argument. Do not make every section sell everything at once.

### Active and direct language
Prefer short, active constructions. Remove unnecessary qualifiers and filler.

### Honest persuasion
Never invent testimonials, statistics, customers, results, certifications, scarcity, guarantees, prices, features, or business facts.

## Message Structure

When appropriate, organize the argument as:

1. **Current situation/problem** — make the reader recognize their situation.
2. **Desired outcome** — show the practical result they want.
3. **Path** — explain how the product makes that result easier or more achievable.
4. **Proof/reassurance** — reduce important objections using real evidence.
5. **Action** — tell the reader exactly what to do next.

For a landing page, normally prioritize:

- Hero: headline + specific subheadline + primary CTA
- Problem/pain
- Solution and benefits
- How it works
- Proof or credibility
- Objection handling / FAQ
- Final CTA

Do not force every section into a page when the available evidence or offer does not support it.

## Headlines

A headline should communicate the strongest relevant value proposition.

Useful patterns:

- Achieve [outcome] without [major pain]
- The [category] for [audience]
- [Desired outcome] without [unwanted complexity]
- Tired of [problem]? [solution/path]
- Create/build/get [specific result] with [mechanism]

Prefer specificity and audience relevance over clever wordplay.

## Subheadlines

The subheadline should clarify the headline by answering some combination of:

- What is this?
- Who is it for?
- How does it work?
- What practical result does it help create?

Keep it concise.

## CTAs

Avoid generic labels when a more concrete action is possible.

Prefer:

- [Action] + [what the user gets]
- [Action] + [specific result]
- See [product] in action
- Create my [specific thing]
- Get the [specific resource]

The CTA must match the actual next step. Never imply a free trial, instant result, download, consultation, or other action unless that is really what happens.

## Ads and Quizzes

For ads, connect the message to the landing-page promise. Do not create a disconnect between the ad and destination.

For quizzes:

1. Attention — identify a recognizable problem or situation.
2. Interest — make the user want to discover their situation/result.
3. Desire — connect the result to a desirable practical outcome.
4. Action — provide a clear next step.

Questions should qualify or educate rather than create friction for its own sake.

## Voice and Tone

Determine the brand voice from the supplied context. Common dimensions:

- casual ↔ formal
- friendly ↔ authoritative
- understated ↔ bold
- technical ↔ accessible
- playful ↔ serious

Keep headlines more forceful when appropriate, but keep body copy easy to understand.

For Brazilian Portuguese, prefer natural Brazilian usage. Avoid literal translations and expressions that sound like generic AI marketing.

## Seu Site Único Context

When the task concerns **Seu Site Único**, preserve these positioning rules unless the user explicitly changes them:

- It is a guided method/platform for creating professional institutional or presentation websites with AI.
- It is not an e-commerce/store builder.
- The audience includes companies, service providers, self-employed professionals, and small businesses.
- The central value is guided construction: the user receives context, prompts, steps, checklists, and direction instead of facing a blank AI chat.
- Desired outcomes commonly include a more professional online presence, more contacts/leads, and better opportunities to attract customers.
- Never promise guaranteed customers, sales, rankings, or revenue.
- Do not invent demonstrations, testimonials, metrics, integrations, features, or results.

## Quality Check

Before delivering copy, verify:

- Is the primary message immediately understandable?
- Is the audience obvious?
- Is the desired outcome concrete?
- Does each section have one job?
- Are benefits connected to real features?
- Is the language natural for the target audience?
- Are vague buzzwords removable?
- Are claims supported by supplied or verified evidence?
- Is the CTA specific and accurate?
- Does the message match the traffic source and destination?
- Does the copy avoid unnecessary hype and fabricated proof?

## Output

When the user asks for finished copy, provide the usable copy first, organized by the requested asset or page sections.

When useful, provide a small number of alternatives for headlines or CTAs. Explain strategic reasoning only when it helps the user make a decision or when requested.

For existing copy, preserve the user's valid information and improve the wording rather than inventing new facts.
